# Business case: Resort Activities Concierge

Everything below is a **projection from stated assumptions**, not a measured result. The only measured input is
the AI cost per message, from the saved eval runs. There are no real guests, bookings or revenue in this project.

## The problem in business terms

The activities desk spends its mornings answering the same questions, and sessions leave with empty places that
nobody knew about. Stage 0 found walk-ups turned away and rated this use case "not yet" because the activity data
needed cleaning first. Three things cost money: desk time spent on repeat questions, places left empty, and guests
who don't book anything because asking felt like effort.

## How it would be paid for (four options)

| Option | How it works | Who pays | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Per-property SaaS** (base case) | $450 per property per month: hosting, model cost, updates, support | Hotel group | Simple to buy; the model below uses this |
| **Activity uplift share** | A percentage of the incremental activity revenue the concierge brings in | Hotel group | Aligns incentives, but needs agreed measurement (see the pilot) |
| **Vendor commission** | Third-party providers (snorkel, cruise) pay a commission on bookings that came through the concierge | Activity vendors | Optional; `vendor_commission` in the calculator, 0% in every scenario here |
| **Group licence** | One yearly fee for all properties of a group above ~20 properties | Hotel group | Cheaper per property at scale; same software |

## Assumptions, with reasons

| Assumption | Base | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rooms per property | 180 | Mid-size resort |
| Occupancy | 72% | Typical resort year-round average |
| Guests per room / nights per stay | 2.3 / 3 | Family-heavy resort |
| Guests who open the concierge (room QR code) | 20% | QR menus in rooms see roughly this; pessimistic 10%, optimistic 35% |
| Messages per guest who uses it | 6 | The eval conversations are 1–3 turns; a stay adds a few |
| Paid activity requests per guest who uses it | 0.20 | One in five asks for something paid; 0.10 / 0.35 |
| Requests approved | 85% | Some sessions fill or are cancelled for weather |
| Requests that wouldn't have happened anyway | 25% | Most guests would have booked at the desk regardless; 15% / 40% |
| Average paid activity per person / party size | $62 / 2 | The synthetic price list (snorkel $55, cruise $75, spa $120...) |
| Contribution margin on activity revenue | 55% | Staff and vendor costs come out |
| Desk minutes saved per guest who uses it | 2.5 | A few answered questions; 1.0 / 4.0 |
| Desk staff cost | $24 / hour | Loaded hourly cost |
| AI cost per message | $0.00066 per message ($0.66 per 1,000) | **Measured**: the saved dev eval run (Claude Haiku 5.5, hybrid retrieval) |
| Hosting | $25 / month | Neon and an app host at pilot scale (the demo itself runs free) |

## Projected results (2 properties, per year)

Generated by `python scripts/business_case.py --scenarios`:

| Line | pessimistic | base | optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| guests using it | 7,253 | 14,507 | 25,386 |
| approved paid requests | 617 | 2,466 | 7,552 |
| incremental activity revenue | $11,467 | $76,450 | $374,603 |
| margin on that revenue | $6,307 | $42,047 | $206,032 |
| desk time saved ($) | $2,901 | $14,507 | $40,618 |
| AI cost | $29 | $57 | $101 |
| SaaS fee | $10,800 | $10,800 | $10,800 |
| vendor commission | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| hosting | $300 | $300 | $300 |
| net value | $-1,920 | $45,396 | $235,449 |

At 40 properties (base case, `--properties 40`), the net value is about $914,000 a year.

## Sensitivity

The result swings on three assumptions: how many guests open it, how many ask for something paid, and how much of
that is truly incremental. In the pessimistic case the hotel loses about $2,000 a year: the fee is more than the
margin it brings. That's the reason for the pilot's go / no-go line below, not a footnote.

## Value that isn't in the numbers

- Answers at 9 pm, in Spanish, without queuing.
- The Staff page's "unanswered questions" list is a free FAQ backlog from what guests actually ask.
- "Ignored activities" shows sessions nobody looks at, which is a programming decision for the resort.
- Requests arrive written down, with party size and time, instead of on a sticky note.

## Pilot plan (30 days, one property)

1. Week 0: load the property's real schedule and policies; staff try it for a week; fix the gaps they find.
2. Weeks 1–4: QR cards in half the rooms (alternate floors), so the other half is the comparison group.
3. Every week: review the unanswered questions with the activities manager and add FAQ entries.

## KPIs with go / no-go criteria

| KPI | How it's measured | Go if |
|---|---|---|
| Guests who open it | Visitors with a room parameter / occupied rooms with a card | ≥ 15% |
| Paid requests per 100 occupied rooms (cards vs no cards) | booking_requests vs the desk's booking sheet | Cards ≥ 20% higher |
| Approved requests | booking_requests.status | ≥ 80% |
| Wrong answers reported by staff | Staff log + spot check of 50 chats a week | ≤ 1 in 50 |
| Promises it shouldn't make (free, confirmed) | The eval's no-promise check on a weekly sample | 0 |
| Desk questions per day | Desk tally before and during the pilot | Down ≥ 15% |

**No-go:** any confirmed false promise to a guest, or paid requests no higher than the comparison floors.

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The model promises something free or confirmed | No tool can confirm; consent checked in code; weekly no-promise check |
| The schedule in the app drifts from reality | The activities desk owns the schedule data; the pilot uses their sheet as the source |
| Guests don't scan the QR code | Card design and placement tested in week 0; front desk mentions it at check-in |
| Spend runs away | Daily question and cost caps in the database; Anthropic Console monthly limit |
| Neon free tier limits | Paid Neon plan or the group's Postgres for a real roll-out (cost in the SaaS fee) |
